# Stomatal Measurement Lab
In this lab, we will be making stomatal measurements to study the impact that polyploidy has on stomata size and density. 
We will be looking at two different species of plants that are polyploid complexes: _Achillea millefolium_ (common yarrow) and _Polypodium californicum_.

## Learning Objectives
- Learn how to use a microscope to measure traits
- Learn how to perform a t-test and to visual data in a box plot
- Learn about the importance of polyploidy in plants

## Pre-Lab
1. Read about how to perform a [one and two sampled t-test](https://camilo-mora.github.io/GEO380/the-two-sample-t-test-by-hand.html)
2. Watch how to perform a [stomatal peel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GgHXkoq76F8)
3. Learn how to [measure with a microscope](https://www.microscopeworld.com/measuring-with-a-microscope/)

## Lab Procedures

### Hypotheses
We want to be able to see if we can identify whether our local populations of _Achillea millefolium_ are tetraploid, hexaploid, or octoploid.
Tetraploid stomata have been measured to be around 21.65 - 23.46 μm.
Hexaploid stomata have been measured to be around 23.89 - 25.96 μm.
Octoploid stomata have been measured to be >26.43 μm.

### Stomatal peels
1. Paint nail polish on the underside of a leaf. Wait 5 minutes to dry
2. Calibrate your eyepiece reticle with a stage micrometer.
3. Peel off the nail polish with scotch tape and place on slide.
4. Measure stomata.

### Statistical tests
1. Measure stomata size for 30 stomata of your sample of _Achillea millefolium_, record data on board. 
2. Use a one sampled t-test to test whether this _Achillea millefolium_ is tetraploid, hexaploid, or octoploid.
3. Record class data on board
